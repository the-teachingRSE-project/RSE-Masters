# deRSE27 talk abstract

**Title:** An RSE Learning Environment: Educational Technology Meets the RSE Community (in Glasgow)

**Type:** Talk

**Focus area:** none of the three; general RSE (education and training)

**Authors:** Julian Dehne (lead), Fabian Lochner, Helene S. Holst, Nicholas Del Grosso, Magnus Hagdorn

## Abstract

RSE teaching material is plentiful, and much of it is excellent. It is also fragmented along disciplinary lines: each field reinvents its tooling, conventions and exercises, and the results rarely travel beyond the course they were written for. What is missing is a shared, open environment in which learners actually practise RSE: forming a team, submitting a repository, receiving feedback and reviewing someone else's code. General-purpose learning management systems such as Moodle, Canvas or ILIAS were built for pages and quizzes. An RSE assignment is a notebook that has to run, a repository at a given commit, or a job that needs a GPU, a container or a batch scheduler. The most advanced open-source platform, Oxford RSE's Gutenberg, provides accounts, courses and progress tracking, but the feedback loop between learners remains open there as everywhere else, and so does the question of where the code runs.

We argue that such an environment should be built around open educational resources: OER in, OER out. Existing open material, from Carpentries lessons to institutional notebooks, should enter the environment without being rewritten, and what instructors and learners produce there should leave it again as reusable, openly licensed learning objects, described by a standard that a different course, discipline or platform can read. Learning-technology standards such as IEEE LOM, SCORM/xAPI or H5P were never designed for content that needs hardware, a toolchain and data to be usable, so we ask what a minimal descriptor for runnable RSE material has to add. A shared environment is also a way to bridge communities: in the UK teaching RSE is an established job, in Germany RSEs usually grow into it, and both sides hold half of what is needed.

The RSE Hackathon at the University of Glasgow on 26–27 November 2026, co-organised by the University of Glasgow, de-RSE and the GI, brought teaching and building RSEs together in roughly equal numbers. Its flagship challenge was a thin slice of such an environment: simple user management, group formation, a feedback loop and peer assessment of uploaded RSE artefacts, together with an overall system design to guide further work. The talk presents the outcome of the hackathon: the prototypes the teams built, the architecture and designs of the RSE Learning Environment, what they decided about extending Gutenberg rather than building from scratch, and the open questions on compute, identity, OER metadata and integration with existing LMSs and forges. Participants take away a design they can reuse for their own teaching infrastructure, and an invitation to join the community roadmap that carries the work forward.

This talk is one of three related submissions from the teachingRSE group, and we suggest scheduling them in one session. "Piloting the RSE Master's Curriculum: Experiences, Consequences and New RSE Research Ideas" reports on the courses such an environment has to serve. "RSE Learning Modules and the iBOTS teaching Environment" presents an existing environment and its modules, from which the model learns.
