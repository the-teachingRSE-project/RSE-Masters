# deRSE27 talk abstract

**Title:** An RSE Learning Environment: Educational Technology Meets the RSE Community (in Glasgow)

**Type:** Talk

**Focus area:** none of the three; general RSE (education and training)

**Authors:** Julian Dehne (lead), Fabian Lochner, Helene S. Holst, Nicholas Del Grosso, Magnus Hagdorn

## Abstract

RSE teaching material is plentiful, and much of it is excellent. What is missing is a shared environment in which learners actually practise RSE: forming a team, submitting a repository, receiving feedback and reviewing someone else's code. General-purpose learning management systems such as Moodle or ILIAS were built for self-contained documents and quizzes. An RSE assignment is a notebook that has to run, a repository at a given commit, or a job that needs a GPU or a batch scheduler. The closest existing platform, Oxford RSE's Gutenberg, provides accounts, courses and progress tracking, but the feedback loop between learners remains open there as everywhere else, and so does the question of where the code runs.

Educational technology has thirty years of models and standards for exactly this kind of loop; the RSE community knows the infrastructure the exercises have to run on. We bring the two together. We take a process model of research-based learning, first implemented in the FL-Trail prototype, and map it onto project-based RSE courses, which are already research projects carried out in teams. Four of the model's seven phases justify dedicated software: group formation, structured concept review, reflection during execution, and peer and co-assessment. From these we derive seven modules with explicit interfaces: group formation, artefact submission, peer code review assignment, co-assessment, a reflection log bound to the repository, an execution back-end, and a module descriptor. The descriptor is a cut-down application profile of IEEE LOM that adds what learning technology never needed: hardware, toolchain and data. Along the way we ask whether learning-object standards (LOM, SCORM/xAPI, H5P) apply to RSE content at all.

The model served as the brief for the RSE Hackathon at the University of Glasgow in November 2026. Co-organised by de-RSE and the GI, the hackathon brought practising RSEs, educational technology researchers and lecturers into one room. The talk presents the model, reports which modules the hackathon teams prototyped and what they decided about extending Gutenberg rather than building from scratch, and names the open questions on compute, identity and integration with existing LMSs and forges. Participants take away a decomposition they can reuse for their own teaching infrastructure, and an invitation to join the consortium that carries the work forward.

This talk is one of three related submissions from the teachingRSE group, and we suggest scheduling them in one session. "Piloting the RSE Master's Curriculum: Experiences, Consequences and New RSE Research Ideas" reports on the courses such an environment has to serve. "RSE Learning Modules and the iBOTS teaching Environment" presents an existing environment and its modules, from which the model learns.
