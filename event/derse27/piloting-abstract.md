# deRSE27 talk abstract

**Title:** Piloting the RSE Master Curriculum: Experiences, Consequences and new RSE Research ideas

**Type:** Talk

**Focus area:** 2 — RSE Research: Software Engineering Research on Research Software (Engineering)

**Authors:** Marc Schmidt (lead), Julian Dehne, Birte Heinemann, Jan Philipp Thiele, Fredo Erxleben, Guido Juckeland

## Abstract

The teachingRSE project has drafted curricular recommendations for a Master's programme in Research Software Engineering. Recommendations alone say little about whether the proposed modules can be taught as described, and how students take up RSE once it is taught to them: whether they recognise it as a career path, whether it changes how they see the software they write for their own theses, and where they draw the line between RSE and software engineering. Before a full programme exists, we therefore pilot individual modules as regular courses at several sites.

In 2026, the pilots covered research paradigms and methods for RSEs and RSE management and communication at RWTH Aachen, an RSE tooling course in Dresden and a computing course in Braunschweig, followed by Responsible RSE at the University of Potsdam in the winter term 2026/27. The pilots are accompanied by a mixed-methods study. Each course starts with the same questionnaire on study background, programming experience, collaborative practice with git, issues and pull requests, and the perception of RSE as an established career path. After the course, we conduct semi-structured interviews with two to three students per course. The interview guide covers reflection on research software the students have built themselves, the meaning of RSE as a profession and its difference to software engineering, and the learning offers students miss. Questions on the profession are asked indirectly to limit socially desirable answers; anonymised transcripts are analysed by interpretive summary.

The talk reports what worked and what did not when the modules met real students, and what follows for the curricular recommendations and for the next pilots at HU Berlin in summer 2027. Beyond teaching, the pilots open new lines of RSE research: in the management course, students interviewed practising RSEs about conflicts, management and communication, and the resulting interviews and role-play scenarios are research data on RSE practice in their own right. Lecturers planning RSE courses take away a reusable survey and interview instrument, and we invite further institutions to run it in their own courses.

This talk is one of three related submissions from the teachingRSE group, and we suggest scheduling them in one session. It sets out the curriculum and what piloting taught us. "RSE Learning Modules and the iBOTS teaching Environment" shows how such modules are built and delivered in practice. "An RSE Learning Environment: Educational Technologies meets the RSE community (in Glasgow)" models the infrastructure in which learners practise RSE together.
